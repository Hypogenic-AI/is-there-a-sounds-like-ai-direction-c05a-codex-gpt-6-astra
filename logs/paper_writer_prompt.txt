# Is there a “sounds like AI” direction in the residual stream?

Research execution: 2026-10-04. **Outcome: partial causal style control; the stronger distinct, detector-consistent, content-preserving hypothesis was not established.**

## 1. Executive Summary

We tested whether a human/AI readout direction also controls new writing, rather than stopping at probe accuracy. In Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, subtracting a matched-corpus direction at layer 24 lowered a blinded Gemini judge’s AI-likeness rating by **20.04/100 points** (95% paired bootstrap CI −22.85 to −17.27; Holm-adjusted sign-flip p = 0.00020). This exceeded the change under five equal-norm random directions. However, coherence fell **0.591/5 points** (CI −0.754 to −0.427), failing the prespecified preservation margin. A frozen RoBERTa detector did not corroborate the primary improvement: its AI score changed **+0.061** (CI −0.072 to +0.193; adjusted p = 0.381). Positive steering increased judge-rated AI-likeness while sharply *decreasing* the encoder’s score.

The readout was strong in both pretrained base and instruction checkpoints: held-out GPT-4o/human AUROC was **0.9707 vs 0.9704**, respectively. It transferred to Llama-3 instruction outputs (instruction-backbone AUROC 0.913), but not Llama-3 base outputs (0.486). Removing five nuisance directions retained a smaller judge-rated style effect, with continuing quality loss and detector disagreement. These results support a controllable component of instruction-model writing style under this protocol, not a universal representation of machine authorship or a demonstrated separation from the published Assistant Axis.

The study produced **1,472 genuine local-model task outputs**, 288 nuisance outputs, two independent scoring systems, 64 repeated judge ratings, and complete cached artifacts. The practical lesson is that readout transfer, generation controllability, detector validity and preservation of meaning must be tested separately. A lower detector score alone was particularly misleading here.

## 2. Research Question, Motivation and Literature

**Question.** Does a direction fitted to human versus machine text causally alter independent judgments of new generations while preserving content and coherence? Is it separable from length, domain, fluency, formality and assistant persona, and does instruction tuning amplify it?

Quaremba et al. and SV-Detect establish strong representation-based detection, but do not establish generation control. Kuznetsov et al. already steer individual SAE features associated with style; their factual side effects prevent claiming that this is the first causal AI-style intervention. The Assistant Axis concerns persona and is an important alternative explanation. The contribution here is the combined intervention/control/measurement test and its negative findings, not a new detector leaderboard result. The full gathered synthesis and paper-reading record are in [literature_review.md](literature_review.md) and [papers/README.md](papers/README.md).

Three directions were retained before experiments: causal dose response; nuisance distinctness; domain/generator/base–instruct transfer. SAE atlases, evasion optimization, fine-tuning, circuit discovery and multilingual universality were pruned. [planning.md](planning.md) preserves the ranked handoff and frozen feasible protocol, including implementation adjustments.

### Data, grouping and controls against leakage

We used the pre-gathered HAP-E release, joining human **chunk 2** and generated continuations by source ID. Human chunk 1 was not mislabeled as a comparison response. Academic/blog/news sources were split into **384 training, 96 validation and 128 test documents**; **128** fiction/spoken/TV-movie documents formed a held-out-domain set. Training used only GPT-4o versus human continuations. Test sets additionally contained Llama-3-8B base and instruction continuations. Each four-way test source thus shares its human reference; it is not four independent documents. Another **128 HC3 question pairs** tested task transfer without fitting any direction.

The resulting corpus contains **2,240 texts**. Texts were reduced to the first 128 whitespace-delimited words, then at most 192 model tokens. Source IDs and normalized text hashes were disjoint across folds. A training-vocabulary character-4-gram TF-IDF audit found maximum test-to-training cosine **0.580**, with no cases above 0.90. This does not exclude semantic overlap or pretraining contamination. All exact source IDs, labels, text excerpts and hashes are saved in `results/corpus.json`; data revisions are in the original resource manifests. Length matching is approximate, not guaranteed by truncation.

Generation used **64 distinct fictitious factual records**, eight variants in each of eight community-event topics. Prompts requested a 70–100-word paragraph retaining supplied facts without additional claims. Records specify location, event, day/time, coordinator, an item count, free attendance and a registration deadline. They are test inputs assembled procedurally, not fabricated model behavior. Exact inputs and full prompts are in [results/tasks.json](results/tasks.json) and `results/generations.jsonl`. Their repeated template limits the diversity of the 64 inferential units.

### Extraction, vector fitting and intervention

Raw response text, without chat framing, was passed through each checkpoint. Hooks captured the **last real token after transformer blocks 8, 16 and 24**, one-based, before final normalization. Left padding kept this token at the last position. We fitted a unit AI-minus-human mean direction and an L2 logistic normal (`C=1`, max_iter=2000) directly in native coordinates, with no PCA or scaling inversion. Layer 24 won validation mean-direction AUROC in both checkpoints (0.9811 instruction; 0.9825 base). Generation scores were not used for layer selection.

For a unit vector `v`, addition was `h' = h + α σ v`, where σ is the training standard deviation of projection onto the original mean vector: 18.811 instruction and 19.704 base. We tested α = −2, −1, 0, +1, +2 in the instruction model. The ±2 addition norms were about 23–24% of mean training last-token residual norm. All random and nuisance additions used the **same absolute norm**, not their own projection SD. Ablation was uncentered `h' = h − (h·v)v`, which is not equivalent to negative addition.

The hook changed only the last prompt token predicting the first output and each subsequent cached generation token. Earlier prompt positions were untouched. Arithmetic checks, unchanged earlier positions, zero-hook generation equivalence and repeated sampling smoke tests passed for both models. These checks test the implemented intervention, not the causal validity of the chosen layer.

Conditions comprised zero, four signed mean doses, logistic −2, ablation, five random −2 directions, formality −2, assistant-proxy −2, nuisance-residualized ±2, and the plain “write like a human” prompt. All 17 instruction conditions used seed 42; zero and mean ±2 also used seed 43. The base model ran zero and mean ±2 with seed 42. Sampling was temperature 0.7, top_p 0.9, top_k 0, max_new_tokens 160. Batch seeds were the stated seed plus the starting batch offset. The instruction model used its chat template; the base model used `Instruction: …\nResponse:`. Consequently, generation differences between checkpoints are not a clean isolated tuning effect.

### Nuisance estimation and independent measurements

Each checkpoint generated 24 paired texts for each of formal/casual, verbose/concise and helpful-assistant/fictional-narrator prompts on separate pottery-course records (144 outputs per checkpoint). Greedy generation held supplied facts fixed, but generated realizations could differ in content. Last-token mean differences provided three nuisance proxies. A training activation/length covariance vector and academic-versus-other-domain mean vector supplied two more. QR orthogonalization removed their joint span from the AI direction; the remainder was normalized. **The assistant control is a reduced output-text proxy, not a reproduction of Lu et al.’s multi-role Assistant Axis.**

Two external measures saw only generated text: the frozen RoBERTa detector’s ChatGPT probability and a blinded Gemini style score (0–100). Gemini additionally rated coherence (1–5), source-fact coverage (0–1), and unsupported-claim fraction (0–1). Requests shuffled conditions into batches of eight and replaced all condition/model IDs with opaque integers. The full rubric is in [results/judge_rubric.json](results/judge_rubric.json). Encoder and judge share neither fitted activations nor intervention labels, but statistical independence of their errors is not assumed. We also recorded token/word counts, truncation, repeated trigrams, script-mixing flags and simple anchor recall. Anchor substring recall is only a rough check, not semantic fact verification.

The detector’s model card states that it trained on **all HC3**. Therefore, encoder validity was assessed on **64 held-out HAP-E human/GPT-4o pairs**, not HC3. These 64 IDs were lexicographically selected from the held-out test split and do not represent every domain equally. Judge calibration used the same pairs. A separate 64-output re-rating checked judge reliability under different batching.

### Statistical plan and decision rules

The primary contrast was instruction mean −2 versus zero on both external style scores. Seeds were averaged **within prompt**, then 64 prompts were bootstrapped 5,000 times for percentile 95% intervals. Paired sign-flip tests used 10,000 draws, with Holm correction across the two primary style tests. We report paired standardized effect `dz` and all tested conditions. Sign-flip inference assumes exchangeability/symmetry under the paired null; bootstrap intervals avoid a normal-error model. Ordinal quality scores are treated as numeric rubric averages. Other contrasts are exploratory with unadjusted tests, not additional confirmed discoveries.

Preservation required lower 95% confidence bounds above **−0.25 coherence points** and **−0.05 fact-coverage units**. Failure to establish noninferiority is not automatically proof of damage: the coherence result is clear damage, while some content intervals are inconclusive. Coverage alone also does not rule out invented claims, so unsupported-claim scores remain essential. We report the full curves, then descriptive comparisons restricted to mutually high-quality, matched-coherence outputs; those post-treatment subsets cannot identify an unbiased causal effect. Eight-template cluster resampling, individual-seed results and length-adjusted regression are exploratory robustness checks.

### Error analysis and cost

The primary instruction baseline already contained unsupported claims: average unsupported fraction was 0.179, and only 14.8% of outputs met the requested 70–100-word range. Mean −2 raised the proportion rated below coherence 4 from **5.5% to 21.9%**. Mean +2 produced detected Chinese/Cyrillic-script mixing in **3.9%** of outputs, versus 1.6% at zero, and a 2.3% truncation rate. These flags describe errors and were never used to discard outputs.

For the first library task, the zero output invented an anniversary; mean −2 invented an end time and duration, calling “13:00” noon; mean +2 added ornate promotion and a Chinese ending. This fixed example was not chosen for favorable steering. Other failure classes include unsupported target audiences or refreshments, omitted registration deadlines, exaggerated claims, and awkward quantity descriptions. Full fixed-prompt comparisons and the lowest-rated cases are in `results/paired_examples.json` and `results/failure_examples.json`; all outputs remain available.

## 5. Discussion

The intervention is causally effective on generated wording and on one independent model’s perception of style. Signed doses, zero-hook checks, equal-norm random controls and repeated seeds make a pure readout-only account inadequate for this specific checkpoint and task. Nevertheless, the central **conjunction**—consistent independent detection, content/coherence preservation and distinctness—failed. Direction sign was consistent with the judge but often reversed under the encoder; high doses introduced collateral changes. The result should not be presented as validated detector evasion or as evidence that outputs became human-authored.

The transfer failure on base-generator text is especially informative. A direction that separates GPT-4o from human continuations can resemble an instruction-generation style axis without detecting all machine production. Its presence in a pretrained backbone shows that the representation does not require chat tuning in that backbone, but does not establish an internal explicit belief about authorship. Near-equal base/instruction readout performance provides no evidence of amplification under the tested metric.

Residualization weakens but does not eliminate the judged style effect. Our nuisance measurements are incomplete and partly content-contaminated; removing their span is not a semantic guarantee. The evidence therefore leaves open whether a better layer/vector/model, a richer Assistant Axis estimate, or a lower-dose operating point could yield more selective control. None should be inferred from these data without a new controlled test.

## 6. Limitations and Deviations

- **Restricted models/tasks.** One 1.5B model family, historical checkpoints, 64 templated announcement records, and no human annotators. Base and instruction generation formats differ. Strong conclusions about modern frontier models, free continuation or a universal axis are unsupported.
- **Measurement weakness.** The historical encoder transferred poorly; the current judge had substantial re-rating noise and judged both style and quality. Its provenance discrimination on HAP-E does not validate every subtle treatment comparison. Factual coverage does not exclude invented information.
- **Quality and dose.** Strong interventions were large relative to residual norm. The baseline did not reliably obey length or no-extra-facts instructions. Matched-coherence filtering conditions on treatment outcomes and is descriptive only. Failure of a noninferiority test can reflect insufficient precision.
- **Incomplete distinctness.** The assistant proxy is not the published Assistant Axis; no dedicated fluency intervention, exhaustive persona subspace, crossed persona/style prompt experiment, or full formality manipulation validation was run. Nuisance output pairs may differ in facts. Length/NLL diagnostics do not resolve all confounding.
- **Reduced handoff scope.** The frozen execution appendix reduced the aspirational ≥300 prompts/three seeds/multiple families to 64 prompts, two primary seeds and a paired base checkpoint. No SAE analysis, shuffled-label intervention, full RAID/MAGE stress test, independent likelihood detector, or causal generation test on natural HAP-E continuations was run. Domain/generator transfer here is **readout transfer**, not causal transfer to new tasks. These omissions are not claimed as completed experiments.
- **Inference/reproducibility.** Most contrasts are exploratory; only two primary score tests received Holm correction. Prompt bootstrap assumes records are exchangeable despite common templates; eight-cluster sensitivity is limited. Exact token output may depend on GPU/software versions. API re-ratings are not deterministic; cached analysis is reproducible. Full inference was not rerun twice, but repeated smoke batches and the complete numerical analysis were checked.
- **Contamination/selection.** Source and lexical audits cannot exclude model pretraining contamination. HAP-E itself filters refusals/incomplete text. HC3 is older and stylistically confounded. Calibration was a small lexicographically selected held-out subset. Corpus matching holds prefix/topic, not every proposition, constant.

## 7. Conclusions and Next Steps

A human/AI residual readout direction **can change writing and judge-perceived AI-like style** in this setup. We did **not** establish a distinct, detector-consistent AI-ness direction that preserves coherence and content: the primary steering damaged coherence, the two measures disagreed, and transfer to base-generator text failed.

The next decisive study should retain signed/random/nuisance/prompt controls while using a stronger contemporary paired base/instruction family, diverse naturally grounded writing tasks, human ratings plus a detector validated on current generators, and a genuine multi-role Assistant Axis estimate. Preselect layer/dose on separate quality-aware validation data and test preservation on a larger untouched set. Report nulls and measurement disagreement rather than optimizing the evaluation detector.

## Shared protocol
1. Begin with an accessible 1.7–4B instruction model; Qwen3-1.7B is supported by SV-Detect backbone ablations. Pin revision and disable thinking where appropriate. Gemma-2-2B is a useful alternative if weights are accessible; its published SAE results do not transfer feature indices to another model. Inspect GPU before choosing dtype/batch size. Model weights are not part of this resource-gathering deliverable.
2. Use HAP-E human chunk 2 versus model continuation, joined by document ID; chunk 1 is generation context only. HC3 same-question answers provide second genre/task. Neither corpus guarantees identical propositions across human/AI answers: pair matching controls prompt/topic, not content. Keep raw and cleaned versions. Remove explicit author/model/template identifiers by a fixed rule; record rather than silently drop refusals.
3. Split by original source document/question **before** tokenization or fitting directions; keep every generator, continuation and attack variant of a source in the same fold. Detect duplicate/near-duplicate material across HC3 and MAGE (MAGE incorporates existing corpora); cross-corpus labels do not guarantee independence. Fit PCA, normalization, nuisance bases, directions and detector thresholds on training/calibration only. Freeze layer/scale selection on validation before final test.
4. Extract response-only, attention-mask-aware residuals, retaining last-real-token and mean-token alternatives. Record hook location (post-block/post-MLP and pre-final-norm), layer index convention, template and tokenizer. Never pool padding or prompt tokens unintentionally. Probe AUROC does not select the most causal layer by itself.
5. Estimate AI-minus-human difference of means and L2 logistic normals at each prespecified layer. If using reduced/standardized features, map the weight back to original residual coordinates (PCA transpose and inverse scaling); bias is not a steering vector. Orient sign with training label means. All-layer detector score is not a single physical residual-stream vector.

## Direction 1: causal dose response
Pilot on ~128 held-out prompts, then aim for >=300 independent source prompts, 3 generation seeds. Use benign fact-constrained explanations and source-grounded rewrites, supplemented by HAP-E continuations. Hold prompts, decoding, max tokens and seed pairs constant across conditions. For HAP-E continuation content is not fixed, so analyze separately.

Choose a small middle/late layer grid on validation. Normalize v to unit norm and scale additions in training projection-SD units: h' = h + alpha * sd(<h,v>) * v, alpha in {-2,-1,-0.5,0,0.5,1,2}. Record relative residual norm as well; stop scales yielding pilot collapse by a prespecified coherence criterion. Apply to generated tokens; explicitly define whether the final prompt state predicting the first token is intervened on and ensure cached decoding matches that choice. Compare fixed negative addition with projection removal h' = h - <h,v>v; those are different interventions. Include zero, shuffled-label directions, >=5 norm-matched random directions, and style-prompt baselines.

Primary outcome: paired change in frozen independent detector score on generated text, excluding prompt. Use a separately trained encoder detector and a different-family likelihood/curvature detector (Fast-DetectGPT) with held-out calibration. Freeze them before steering selection; do not tune against the final detector. If detectors disagree, report disagreement. Do not equate a lower detector score with actual human authorship.

Joint outcomes: factual proposition retention/QA accuracy against supplied source, blinded coherence and instruction-following ratings, repetition and truncation/EOS, and output token counts. Embedding similarity and perplexity are supporting metrics only. Predeclare noninferiority margins after pilot (e.g. <=5 percentage-point factual-accuracy loss, <=0.25 loss on a 1–5 coherence rubric), then freeze before test. Report the full style-versus-quality curve and the fraction meeting BOTH constraints; never select only fluent successes post hoc.

## Direction 2: distinctness and nuisance controls
Build matched prompts/texts for formal–informal, concise–verbose, assistant–nonassistant, and domain contrasts with content held fixed as far as possible. Use multiple nuisance prompts and independent source groups. Estimate all nuisance directions in the SAME model/layer coordinate system. Compare cosine alignment and a joint orthonormal nuisance basis Q from training data; v_resid = (I - QQ^T)v. Report removed norm. If the residual norm is negligible, do not normalize numerical noise.

Compare original, residualized, each nuisance-only direction and a norm-matched random residualized direction with identical dose and quality gates. Match text length within domain at training; report generation outcomes in length strata but do not rely solely on post-treatment length regression (length may mediate treatment). A 2x2 crossed style/persona prompt design tests whether the effect persists beyond assistant framing. Geometry alone neither proves nor refutes causal independence; partialling out nuisance directions may remove true style signal too.

## Direction 3: transfer
Train directions on selected HAP-E generators/domains; test held-out source groups, domains and generators; HC3/MAGE/RAID provide additional shifts. RAID human rows must be retained and attack variants linked by source_id/adv_source_id. Analyze chat/base sources separately and clean/attacked text separately. Estimate direction independently in a second model family: raw vectors cannot simply be copied across model coordinates. Reuse the same frozen external detectors and joint quality criteria. A detection transfer result alone is not a causal transfer result.

## Analysis and decision rules
Unit of inference is source prompt/document. Cluster-bootstrap paired differences by source, with all seeds and conditions inside each cluster. Report 95% intervals, domain effects, dose monotonicity and detector agreement. Preregister primary layer/scale comparison after validation; correct or clearly label exploratory layer/dose comparisons. Random-direction controls must share norm and hook. A null result is informative: a readable direction need not control generation. Claim distinct causal AI-style only if signed response survives independent detectors, source/content/coherence controls and nuisance interventions. Otherwise report the narrower result (readout-only, nuisance-mediated, detector-specific, or quality-damaging).


## Motivation & Novelty Assessment (execution preregistration)
### Why This Research Matters
A causal writing-style direction would distinguish readable authorship information from controllable generation behavior. This matters for interpreting detectors and separating surface style from assistant identity.
### Gap in Existing Work
The reviewed probes establish readout, while SAE work already changes individual stylistic features, sometimes corrupting facts. A single direction with independent style measurement and preserved content is not established.
### Our Novel Contribution
Test the same estimated residual normal under signed additions, ablation, nuisance removal, random controls and independent scoring; quantify base/instruct differences without assuming universal AI authorship semantics.
### Experiment Justification
1. Causal signed interventions test whether reading predicts writing control.
2. Formality/persona/verbosity controls test alternative mechanisms.
3. Held-out generators/domains, HC3 and a paired base checkpoint test transfer and tuning effects.

# Is there a “sounds like AI” direction in the residual stream?

## Scope and conclusion

There is strong evidence that human/AI text provenance is linearly readable from frozen LLM representations, and prior work causally steers individual stylistic features. The reviewed evidence does **not establish a single, distinct, content-preserving AI-style generation direction**. The useful contribution is therefore a controlled intervention with independent detectors and explicit nuisance tests, rather than another separability result.

Scope: 2023–2026 work on representation-based detection, causal residual steering, persona geometry, and matched human/AI corpora. Include direct methodological evidence and reusable resources; exclude unrelated watermarking, general-purpose fine-tuning, and detector-evasion optimization. This is a focused resource review, not an exhaustive systematic review. All four specified references were read through all 43 three-page-or-shorter chunks (127 pages); six supplemental papers were screened by abstract/first chunk and selected methods/results. Detailed per-chunk evidence is in [reading_log.md](artifacts/resource_finder/reading_log.md); bibliographic details and PDFs in [papers/README.md](papers/README.md).

## Key papers

### 1. Linear Probing Provides Robust and Efficient Detection of Machine-Generated Text

**Quaremba, Yan, Black, Vrandečić and Simperl, 2026**, [arXiv:2608.24780](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24780), v1; [code](https://github.com/gerritq/mgt_probes). Last-token residuals, 100-component PCA per layer, and logistic probes form layer-averaged (LLP) or concatenated-layer (CLP) detectors. Detection uses DetectRL, MultiSocial, RAID and TSM, 16 settings with 1,500 balanced training and 500 test examples each; M4GT supports geometry analyses. Sixteen baselines include RoBERTa, RepreGuard, TextFluoroscopy, Fast-DetectGPT and Binoculars.

Reported mean OOD improvements range 0.39–11.37 AUROC points. Probe alignment and correlations with editing strength support shared readout structure, not generation causality. The low-label PCA experiment observes the full training set, although a no-PCA appendix supports similar sample efficiency. Authors explicitly leave universality/confounding/causality unresolved. Implementation standardizes before PCA: inverse-map the classifier normal into residual coordinates before intervention.

### 2. SV-Detect: AI-generated Text Detection with Steering Vectors

**Vishnyakov and Gaintseva, 2026**, [arXiv:2606.07313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07313), v2; [code](https://github.com/Atmyre/sv-detect). Frozen GPT-Neo-2.7B mean-pooled states yield per-layer mean-difference, logistic, or paired-PCA directions; cosine features feed a standardized logistic head. DetectRL, MIRAGE and PADBen evaluate domain/generator/editing transfer; RAID/COLING extend evaluation. Metrics include AUROC, AUPR, F1, MCC and TPR at fixed FPR; baselines include score-based detectors, RoBERTa variants and RepreGuard.

Directions are explicitly used **only for probing/classification**. Logistic directions outperform mean/PCA in difficult transfer; regex typography/discourse/lexical baselines leave additional signal unexplained. This does not identify its causal meaning. RAID transfer remains much weaker for non-chat generators; a multi-direction variant reports 56.3% overall TPR at 5% FPR. Near-perfect RAID matched-training results are a different protocol. Code/documentation disagree on vector C and head intercept; [code notes](code/README.md) record exact discrepancies.

### 3. Feature-Level Insights into Artificial Text Detection with Sparse Autoencoders

**Kuznetsov et al., 2025**, [arXiv:2503.03601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03601), v1; [feature explorer](https://mgtsaevis.github.io/mgt-sae-visualization/). Gemma-2-2B/Gemma Scope residual SAE features are summed over tokens and classified with XGBoost; baselines are raw activation XGBoost, individual-feature thresholds and a shared-task multi-task detector. COLING-2025 supplies train/dev/devtest/test and RAID supplies attack analysis. Layer 16 generalizes well by macro-F1, but individual features vary across domains/generators.

Crucially, the paper already steers decoder directions, scaled by reference maximum activation, and interprets 50-generation sets with GPT-4o. Features affect complexity, assertion, verbosity, repetition and formality. Appendix examples show factual corruption at strong doses. The judge interprets features rather than independently measuring detector score and preserved facts. One model/SAE, summed-feature length dependence, source artifacts and all-token intervention limit causal generalization. This is the closest prior intervention work and prevents claiming first AI-style feature steering.

### 4. The Assistant Axis: Situating and Stabilizing the Default Persona of Language Models

**Lu, Gallagher, Michala, Fish and Lindsey, 2026**, [arXiv:2601.10387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10387), v1; [specified article](https://www.anthropic.com/research/assistant-axis), [code](https://github.com/safety-research/assistant-axis). Gemma2-27B, Qwen3-32B and Llama3.3-70B response activations span 275 roles and 240 traits. The assistant-minus-role mean aligns with role-space PC1. Addition changes role adoption; projection capping reduces persona-jailbreak harmful responses by roughly 60% in selected configurations while preserving measured IFEval/MMLU-Pro/GSM8K/EQ-Bench performance.

These are persona/safety outcomes, not AI-writing detection. Synthetic conversations, qualitative persona labels, large-model scope and implementation differences constrain conclusions. Assistant projection is therefore a necessary nuisance control, estimated in the same model/layer as AI-style vectors. Do not transfer published vectors directly to another model, or equate capping, ablation and signed addition.

### 5. Steering Llama 2 via Contrastive Activation Addition

**Rimsky et al., ACL 2024**, [published paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.828/), local `2312.06681.pdf`; [code](https://github.com/nrimsky/CAA). Paired positive/negative answer-letter residual differences are averaged and added with signed coefficients after the prompt. Llama2-7B/13B evaluations cover seven behaviors using multiple-choice and open generation, GPT-4 judging, and capability controls; system prompting and fine-tuning are comparators. It supplies the causal intervention recipe, not evidence about provenance style. Prompt-format dependence and collateral changes motivate random-vector and quality controls.

### 6. Do LLMs write like humans? Variation in grammatical and rhetorical styles

**Reinhart et al., 2024/2025**, [arXiv:2410.16107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16107), v2. HAP-E and CAP compare human continuations with six model continuations from the same context. Biber's 66 linguistic features support random forest/logistic classifiers and paired Wilcoxon tests with multiplicity correction. Seven-source HAP-E classification reaches 66% versus 14% chance; this is not binary detector accuracy. Instruction models show stronger stylistic deviations than base models; cross-model/domain transfer is imperfect. This supports domain/formality/base-versus-chat controls. HAP-E has 8,290 common sources after refusal/incomplete-output filtering; context matching does not equate factual content. Public corpus and linguistic tooling are linked in the paper/card; no separate implementation cloned.

### 7–10. Benchmarks and independent scoring

|Paper|Method, data and evidence|Use and limitations|
|---|---|---|

## Synthesis: methods, baselines and measurement

The common pipeline is frozen activations → pooled representation → learned direction → detector score. Generation intervention adds a separate causal step and can fail even with excellent readout. A classifier normal, mean difference, PCA component and SAE decoder column solve different problems; none is automatically the optimal causal control vector. Multi-layer detection scores are not single-layer vectors.

Minimum baselines: unsteered model; signed mean-difference and logistic-normal interventions; label-shuffled and norm-matched random vectors; formal/informal and concise/verbose prompts; independently estimated assistant/formality/length/domain directions. SAE interpretations inform these controls without adding a fourth experiment. Same-detector improvements alone can reflect detector-specific exploitation.

Report readout AUROC/AUPR and TPR at validation-calibrated FPR. Primary generation outcomes are **paired external score changes jointly with factual retention and coherence**. Add instruction adherence, repetition, output lengths, truncation and detector disagreement. Blinded ratings and source-grounded QA complement semantic similarity; embedding similarity alone cannot establish preserved facts. Use source-cluster bootstrap confidence intervals, seed-paired comparisons, and frozen validation-selected layer/scale settings. Show full score–quality curves rather than discarding incoherent failures.

## Recommendations and unresolved questions

Implement only the three ranked directions in [planning.md](planning.md): (1) signed causal dose response, (2) nuisance/persona separation, (3) held-out domain/generator and second-model transfer. HAP-E is primary, HC3 adds task shift, RAID/MAGE stress generalization. All requested data are local; [dataset notes](datasets/README.md) include label conventions and validated overlaps. An RTX A6000 is available; a small accessible model is a practical first pilot. Model weights and experiment dependencies are not installed.

Split by source before fitting PCA, scalers, directions, nuisance bases or detectors. HAP-E human chunk 1 is context only; HC3 indices are nonunique; MAGE labels are inverted relative to AI=1 and its validation/test files overlap; RAID variants share source IDs. Published provenance labels and frozen detectors operationalize style but do not define a universal essence of AI writing.

A distinct causal-style claim requires effects surviving independent scoring, factual/coherence constraints, nuisance interventions and transfer. A null or narrower result—readout-only, persona-mediated, detector-specific or quality-damaging—is scientifically useful. Orthogonalization alone is not proof of semantic independence, and removing a nuisance subspace can remove genuine style signal. No causal outcome has been measured in this resource phase.

   \input{commands/general}
   \input{commands/macros}

5. REFERENCE CONVENTIONS:
   Use reference macros from math.tex:
   - \figref{fig:name} for "figure 1" (lowercase, in-sentence)
   - \Figref{fig:name} for "Figure 1" (capitalized, start of sentence)
   - \secref{sec:name} for "section 2"

6. TEXT FORMATTING:
   - Use \para{Header text} for bold paragraph headers
   - Define method/dataset names with \textsc and \xspace:
     \newcommand{\methodname}{\textsc{MethodName}\xspace}

7. TABLE FORMATTING:
   - Use booktabs package (no vertical lines)
   - Use \resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{...} for wide tables
   - Use @{} to remove padding at table edges
   - Use \cmidrule(lr){x-y} for sub-headers
   - Use \textsc{} for dataset/method names in headers
   - Bold best results with {\bf ...}

8. FIGURE FORMATTING:
   - Use 0.32\textwidth for 3-column subfigures
   - Use 0.95\linewidth for full-width figures
   - Use \input{figures/legend} for shared legends
   - Write self-contained captions explaining key observations

9. RESULTS PRESENTATION:
   - Define \increase and \decrease for colored arrows (green up, red down)
   - Bold best results in tables
   - Report confidence intervals when available

10. ALGORITHM STYLING:
    - Use algpseudocode with [noend]
    - Use \triangleright for comments

11. HYPERLINKS (REQUIRED):
    - Always use \usepackage[hidelinks]{hyperref} or with colored links
    - All citations, section refs, figure refs, table refs must be clickable
    - This is essential for reader navigation
