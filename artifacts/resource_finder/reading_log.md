## Chunk reading log (will be archived in the final synthesis)
- 2503.03601 chunk 001, pp.1–3: Gemma-2-2B Gemma Scope SAE, summed token features and XGBoost; comparison to mean-pooled raw activations; individual-feature thresholds; COLING train/dev/devtest/test and RAID. Steering adds lambda times reference maximum activation times decoder column. Layer 16 generalizes well; not evidence yet of content-preserving global AI-ness control.
- 2503.03601 chunk 002, pp.4–6: Features 3608 complexity, 4645 assertiveness, 6587 wordy introduction, 8264 repetition, 14161 formality. Essay/review domains undermine universality. One model/SAE and interpretation uncertainty limit generality; references include HC3, MAGE, RAID.
- 2503.03601 chunk 003, pp.7–9: Appendix documents source-specific punctuation and length artifacts. RAID analysis restricts to ChatGPT/GPT-4, 100 continuations per combination. Length sensitivity compares top/bottom deciles within domains; anomaly-sensitive features intersect across domains.
- 2503.03601 chunk 004, pp.10–12: Actual human and AI punctuation artifacts shown. Attack-sensitive features mostly differ from detector-important features, but intersection procedure can miss domain-dependent confounds. Steering operates across all token positions unlike naturally sparse activation; this is a key causal interpretation limitation.
- 2503.03601 chunk 005, pp.13–15: Appendix G uses signed shifts from -4 to +4 (excluding zero in listed grid); GPT-4o interprets changes. Steering can introduce hallucinations and alter information density; natural examples and intervention interpretations do not always agree.
- 2503.03601 chunk 006, pp.16–18: Tables 2–5 distinguish surface-attack latents from style/discourse latents; both overly positive and negative steering damage readability or completeness. Feature 8264 affects repetition; 8689 synonym substitution.
- 2503.03601 chunk 007, pp.19–21: Table 7 examples explicitly show factual content changes under strong steering (e.g. invented Neil Armstrong biography); this prevents treating the existing results as content-preserving AI-ness control. Model-specific F1 varies substantially.
- 2503.03601 chunk 008, p.22: Full GPT interpretation prompt uses original/modified sequences, 50 generations, and requests semantic/stylistic/structural descriptions. It is a feature-interpretation judge, not an independent AI detector or blinded content-preservation evaluation.
- 2606.07313 chunk 001, pp.1–3: v2 explicitly uses directions only for probing/classification, not generation. Mean pooling per layer; normalized mean difference, logistic normal, or paired PCA; official code https://github.com/Atmyre/sv-detect/.
- 2606.07313 chunk 002, pp.4–6: Layer cosine features -> StandardScaler -> logistic regression (liblinear, L2, C=1, seed 42). GPT-Neo-2.7B, 2048 tokens. DetectRL balanced subsampling, MIRAGE 500 polishing pairs, PADBen transfer; RAID/COLING supplements. Baselines include likelihood, rank, entropy, LRR/NPR, DetectGPT, Fast-DetectGPT, DNA-GPT, Binoculars, RoBERTa, RepreGuard. AUROC/AUPR/TPR@5%FPR/accuracy/MCC/F1.
- 2606.07313 chunk 003, pp.7–9: Logistic direction is more robust than mean/PCA in worst transfer; source-dependent layer contributions; lexical/style surface features explain some but not all classification performance. Residual unexplained signal is not proof of independent causal AI-ness. Strong detection and transfer; no controlled generation outcome.
- 2606.07313 chunk 004, pp.10–12: Limitations: supervised labels, English coverage, evolving generators, full LLM pass costs, document-level attribution. References provide MIRAGE/DetectRL/PADBen sources; claim scope remains detection.
- 2606.07313 chunk 005, pp.13–15: A100 40GB FP16 latency test, 64 texts capped at 512 tokens; full forward cost dominates. Backbone comparison includes Qwen3-1.7B/Base and Gemma3-1B pt/it; Qwen base strongest worst-case transfer in this setup, not universal evidence.
- 2606.07313 chunk 006, pp.16–18: Appendix style baseline covers typography, markdown, discourse, lexical cues, template leakage and midword case flips, normalized per 1000 characters. Logit-lens rankings explicitly noisy; layer contribution differs between MIRAGE and DetectRL. Need independent nuisance controls beyond this finite regex bank.
- 2606.07313 chunk 007, pp.19–21: COLING English totals 610,767 train, 261,758 dev, 73,941 test; trimming weak generator families helps Llama-2 but hurts GPT-Neo. Added task-specific directions and QR basis are detection extensions, not a scalar universal axis.
- 2606.07313 chunk 008, pp.22–24: RAID zero-shot transfer is weak for base/non-chat generators; task-aware multi-direction detector reaches 56.3% overall TPR at 5% FPR, 78.9% on chat generators. Tables distinguish threshold-sensitive F1 from AUROC; preserve that distinction in replication.
- 2606.07313 chunk 009, pp.25–27: Full mean/PCA/logistic and classifier ablations; RAID matched-supervision results much stronger than cross-benchmark transfer. RepreGuard strongly backbone-sensitive. Do not compare matched-supervision ~99.7% TPR with zero-shot transfer as equivalent protocols.
- 2608.24780 chunk 001, pp.1–3: Quaremba et al. probe frozen residual activations; shared separability across domains is observational. Four representation quality measures and shallow/deep MLP comparison; official code gerritq/mgt_probes.
- 2608.24780 chunk 002, pp.4–6: Last-token states, training-only top-100 PCA per layer, L2 logistic C=1. LLP averages per-layer normalized projections; CLP concatenates reduced layers. Llama-3-8B; 1500 train/500 test balanced per each of 16 settings across DetectRL/MultiSocial/RAID/TSM. Must map PCA weights back before residual intervention.
- 2608.24780 chunk 003, pp.7–9: OOD means improvements 0.39–11.37 AUROC points; last-layer vector similarity and edit-strength correlations, not generation causality. Sample-efficiency PCA is fit on full training set (footnote 7), so 10–100 label claim is not 10–100 total observed examples. Authors explicitly leave causality, universality, confounding open.
- 2608.24780 chunk 004, pp.10–12: References include CAA, Fast-DetectGPT, Binoculars, representation geometry, DetectRL and TSM. No intervention result introduced here.
- 2608.24780 chunk 005, pp.13–15: Appendix defines entropy/effective rank/anisotropy/intrinsic dimension; domain-separated clusters do not establish a unique causal direction. Related-work references finish before quantitative appendices.
- 2608.24780 chunk 006, pp.16–18: M4GT for representation analysis, harder four-benchmark suite for detection; 16 baselines described including RepreGuard, TextFluoroscopy, EditLens, RADAR and RoBERTa. APT-Eval/EditLens provide edited-text spectrum; their edit-distance correlation alone does not measure authorship essence.
- 2608.24780 chunk 007, pp.19–21: A100/H200 compute; appendix repeats sample-efficiency without PCA, reducing concern about full-training PCA. Length-specific training is used in length ablation, not a within-topic confound elimination. Shared directions only approximately parallel.
- 2608.24780 chunk 008, pp.22–24: Preliminary 3-way edited-text classification calibrated on 200 validation cases; last-token pooling typically beats mean, averaging layers beats endpoints; no-PCA comparable. Model-size ablations suggest Qwen alternatives feasible. No generation intervention tested.
- 2601.10387 chunk 001, pp.1–3: Lu, Gallagher, Michala, Fish, Lindsey (2026). Gemma2-27B/Qwen3-32B/Llama3.3-70B; 275 roles, 5 prompts x 240 extraction questions, roleplay judge, mean post-MLP residuals over response tokens. 1200 default-assistant rollouts. Code safety-research/assistant-axis.
- 2601.10387 chunk 002, pp.4–6: Assistant axis = default-assistant mean minus mean fully-roleplaying role vectors, not necessarily PC1; 240 trait prompts give converging geometry. Role-space PCs explain 19.4–33.6% of natural assistant activation variation. Same layer/model needed for overlap comparison.
- 2601.10387 chunk 003, pp.7–9: Causal addition at every token in middle layer, scale relative to average post-MLP norm. Tests role susceptibility and persona jailbreaks; strong negative steering induces mystical or fabricated identities, positive can reduce jailbreak compliance. Outcomes are persona/safety, not AI-text detector scores.
- 2601.10387 chunk 004, pp.10–12: Base-model steering increases professional/helpful human archetypes; coding/writing conversations retain assistant projection more than self-reflection/therapy contexts. Topic and persona are entangled candidates, not orthogonal by construction.
- 2601.10387 chunk 005, pp.13–15: Activation capping is a lower-bound projection clamp h - v min(<h,v>-tau,0), unlike fixed addition or total ablation. Calibrated to role-response percentiles; 25th percentile and middle-late multi-layer ranges give reported ~60% harmful-response reduction with benchmark preservation. IFEval/MMLU-Pro/GSM8K/EQ-Bench evaluate capabilities, not content identity.
- 2601.10387 chunk 006, pp.16–18: Case studies replay identical user messages with capping; show persona/safety changes in jailbreak, delusion-reinforcement and emotional-dependency conversations. Qualitative cases are illustrative, not a randomized estimate of AI-style effects.
- 2601.10387 chunk 007, pp.19–21: Discussion cautions fuzzy behavior labels, three accessible models, incomplete persona elicitation and synthetic conversations. A single axis captures only one notion of assistant persona; linearity is not exhaustive.
- 2601.10387 chunk 008, pp.22–24: References and reproducible role-generation template; 5 role instructions, 40 role-specific extraction questions and scoring rubric. Review prompts are data artifacts, not instructions to this assistant.
- 2601.10387 chunk 009, pp.25–27: Trait generation contrasts positive/negative instructions. PCA requires 4/8/19 components for 70% role variation (Gemma/Qwen/Llama). Base/instruct Gemma alignment uses same instruction-model texts, so does not demonstrate equal spontaneous generation behavior.
- 2601.10387 chunk 010, pp.28–30: Trait-space construction subtracts negative from positive response means; filters on judge score difference >=50. Four to seven dimensions explain 70% of trait variance. Trait extraction offers matched nuisance-vector recipe.
- 2601.10387 chunk 011, pp.31–33: Role susceptibility evaluation uses 50 near-assistant roles and five introspective questions; judge categories explicitly separate human/nonhuman/mystical/nonsense/assistant. These identity questions are unsuitable primary prompts for AI-writing style evaluation.
- 2601.10387 chunk 012, pp.34–36: More full examples for all three models show fabricated credentials, metaphors and persona drift under negative steering; measurable output differences need not be stylistic-only.
- 2601.10387 chunk 013, pp.37–39: Full harmful-role evaluation schema and judge prompt specify actionable-compliance categories; independent safety evaluation cannot substitute for independent AI-style detection.
- 2601.10387 chunk 014, pp.40–42: Additional steered examples shift toward refusal, redirection and conventional helpful prose. Such content substitutions could trivially move an AI detector; primary style experiments should use benign content-constrained prompts.
- 2601.10387 chunk 015, pp.43–45: Base-model prefills and Big-Five-like label rubrics; synthetic user personas and conversation topic generation instructions. Provides reusable benign persona extraction questions and illustrates prompt-content confounds.
- 2601.10387 chunk 016, pp.46–48: Full synthetic-user prompt and all model/auditor trajectory plots; writing drift is model-dependent, coding consistently stable. Supports recording prompt domain and content in style experiments.
- 2601.10387 chunk 017, pp.49–51: Qwen capping tradeoff frontier; role-PC1 cosine often >0.6 but effects differ from assistant contrast vector. PC1 does not always represent the same concept in base Llama.
- 2601.10387 chunk 018, pp.52–54: PC1 trajectories and capping comparisons; vLLM implementation scored 2–3% lower than Transformers, a documented implementation confound. Contrast vector is recommended over assuming PC1 equals assistant persona.
- Supplemental screening: CAA first chunk pp.1–3 and selected methods/results. Paired A/B answer-letter residual differences; seven behavioral tasks; Llama2-7B/13B chat; signed addition after prompt. Compare prompting/fine-tuning; open generation judged with GPT-4 and multiple-choice behavior. Transfer to AI-style remains untested by this paper.
- Supplemental screening: RAID first chunk pp.1–3 plus evaluation sections. Original paper 6M+ generations, 11 generators, 8 domains, 4 decoding configurations, 11 attacks; current dataset expanded. Evaluate 12 detectors using low-FPR sensitivity and stratified outcomes. Decoding and generator shifts expose brittleness.
- Supplemental screening: HC3 first chunk pp.1–3 and detector section. English 24,322 questions, 58,546 human and 26,903 ChatGPT answers; Chinese separate corpus. GLTR logistic, RoBERTa single-text and QA pair baselines, F1 on full/filtered/sentence variants. Old single generator and formatting differences limit generalization.
- Supplemental screening: MAGE first chunk pp.1–3 and methods/results. 27 LLMs from 7 families, 10 source corpora and 8 testbeds; Longformer, GLTR/FastText, DetectGPT plus human/ChatGPT judging. OOD human false positives remain severe; small adaptation changes protocol, so separate zero-shot from adapted results.
- Supplemental screening: HAP-E paper first chunk pp.1–3 and methods/results. 8290 shared source documents, six generators; Biber 66 features with paired Wilcoxon/Bonferroni and random forest/logistic classification. Instruction models differ more than base models; source classification 66% vs 14% chance, not a binary AI accuracy. Cross-corpus/LLM performance varies. Corpus matches context, not exact generated facts.
- Supplemental screening: Fast-DetectGPT first chunk pp.1–3 and method/evaluation excerpts. Conditional likelihood discrepancy standardized by analytic conditional variance, evaluated on XSum/SQuAD/WritingPrompts and language/domain shifts. Compared likelihood/rank/entropy/LRR/DetectGPT and trained detectors; reported AUROC 0.9887 white-box / 0.9338 ChatGPT/GPT4 black-box average and 340x speedup are setting-specific. Suitable independent scorer when using a frozen model distinct from steered generator; still vulnerable to shifts.
