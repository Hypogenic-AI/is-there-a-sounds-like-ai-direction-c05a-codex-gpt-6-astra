# Human-AI Parallel English Corpus (HAP-E) &#128579;

## Purpose
The HAP-E corpus is designed for comparisions of the writing produced by humans and the writing produced by large language models (LLMs).

The corpus was created by seeding an LLM with an approximately 500-word chunk of human-authored text and then prompting the model to produce an additional 500 words.

Thus, a second 500-word chunk of human-authored text (what *actually* comes next in the original text) can be compared to the text produced by the LLM.

## Usage

Most comparisions, model fitting, classification, etc. will **exclude the human chunk 1 data**, as those were used to prompt LLMs, but should not be consided part of the parallel data. Chunk 1 is provided to facilitate the querying of other/new LLMs and to enable potentially important sanity checking.

## Corpus Compilation

### Text-types
The corpus is intended to approximate the composition of the [Corpus of Contemporary American English (COCA)](https://www.english-corpora.org/coca/help/texts.asp). However, rather than having 8 text-types, the HAP-E corpus contains 6:

1. **Academic**: samples were drawn from a 40,000+ document corpus of open-access academic articles published by Elsevier.
2. **News**: samples were drawn from a 100,000+ document corpus of news articles published online by U.S.-based news organizations.
3. **Fiction**: samples were drawn novels and short stories in the public domain and available on Project Gutenberg.
4. **Spoken**: samples were drawn from a corpus of 100,000 podcast transcriptions. (Note that COCA's spoken text-type comes from transcriptions of unscripted news shows.)
5. **Blogs**: samples were drawn from a corpus of 681,288 posts  from blogger.com.
6. **Television and Movie Scripts**: samples were drawn from 2 different copora of scripts, some of which were converted using OCR.

### The human-generated text

2,000 samples were collected from each of the 6 text-types to create an original corpus of 12,000 texts.

From each of those texts, the first ~1,000 words were extracted. with the condition that the 1,000-word span did not cross a section boundary or a chapter boundary.

Those spans were then split into 2 roughly 500-word chunks, with the chunk boundary occuring at the end of a sentence (or independent clause) as determined by a dependency parse.

### The LLM-generated text

The first chunk of human-authored text was sent to an LLM with the prompt:

> In the same style, tone, and diction of the following text, complete the next 500 words, generate exactly 500 words, and note that the text does not necessarily end after the generated words:

After prompting, the queried LLM sometimes refused to complete the task or returned an incomplete response. Those have all been filtered out, yielding a corpus of 8,290 texts for each LLM.
